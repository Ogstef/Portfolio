DRIVING PHYSICS & SCENE CHALLENGES — SUMMARY
==============================================
(Portfolio project: Next.js + React Three Fiber + Rapier + ecctrl vehicle controller)

1) DIRT vs. ROAD BEHAVING DIFFERENTLY
--------------------------------------
- Reported symptom: car felt different driving on the painted road vs. the open dirt.
- Investigated Ground.tsx, Road.tsx, and the physics collider setup.
- Found Road.tsx is a purely visual decal mesh — no RigidBody/collider of its own.
- The only physical driving surface in the whole scene is Ground.tsx's single collider.
- Conclusion: dirt and road were already physically identical; nothing to "unify."
- The real complaint turned out to be general handbrake instability, not a surface split.
- No code change needed for this specific ask — redirected effort to the actual handbrake bug.

2) HANDBRAKE U-TURNS FLIPPING THE CAR
--------------------------------------
- Symptom: holding the handbrake (space) during a U-turn would flip the car over.
- Added a gated debug logger (PhysicsDebug.tsx) that only prints wheel-level physics
  data while braking, to avoid spamming the console during normal driving.
- Log evidence showed all 4 wheels were configured as brakeWheel — space acted like
  a full brake pedal, not a real handbrake.
- With every wheel locked, there was zero steering grip left to arrest a slide once
  it started, making a full spin-out (and eventual flip) much easier to trigger.
- Fix: removed brakeWheel from the front two wheels — only the rears lock now,
  matching how a real handbrake works.
- Follow-up log evidence then showed ecctrl's default maxBrakeTorque (40) snapped a
  wheel's spin to zero in a single physics step — a discontinuity that spiked that
  wheel's friction impulse 5-10x its neighbors.
- Softened REAR_BRAKE_TORQUE to 12 so the lock ramps over a few frames instead of one.
- Also split TIRE_GRIP into front/rear values later (see challenge 7) once it became
  clear one shared grip constant was hurting the front wheels' launch traction.

3) GUARANTEEING THE CAR CAN NEVER GET STUCK UPSIDE-DOWN
----------------------------------------------------------
- Requirement: even if it does flip, it must never leave the player staring at the
  underside of the world.
- Built VehicleUpright.tsx: a watchdog that only acts if the car stays tipped past
  ~70° for a full 0.6 continuous seconds (long enough to ignore a brief ramp-jump
  tilt that would self-correct on landing).
- When it fires, it snaps the car upright in place, keeping its heading, using the
  same discrete setTranslation/setRotation/setLinvel/setAngvel calls the existing
  manual reset button already used safely.
- Added a camera-side safety net in FollowCamera.tsx as a second layer: the chase
  camera only follows the car's yaw (heading), never its pitch/roll, and both the
  camera and its look target are floored just above ground level.
- Result: the camera itself literally cannot render the underside of the world,
  independent of whatever the car's chassis is doing.
- Also added VehicleYawLimiter.tsx to cap horizontal spin velocity at ~180°/s, since
  logs showed spins running away to 190-260°/s during hard handbrake holds — a real
  U-turn still works, it just can't runaway into a multi-rotation spin-out.

4) A REVERTED FIX: LOCKING ROTATION BROKE NORMAL DRIVING
------------------------------------------------------------
- First attempt at the anti-flip guarantee was harder: lock pitch/roll outright via
  Rapier's enabledRotations, leaving only yaw free.
- Result was much worse than the original bug: even a light throttle tap launched
  the car into the air.
- Root cause: each wheel's independent suspension spring normally resolves uneven
  per-wheel load by letting the body rotate slightly. With rotation locked, the
  solver had nowhere to put that reaction force except straight into linear velocity.
- Immediately reverted the change once confirmed broken, rather than trying to
  tune around it.
- Lesson carried forward: prefer discrete, occasional corrections (a reset-style
  snap) over continuous constraints that fight the wheel/suspension solver every frame.
- This is why VehicleUpright (challenge 3) corrects rarely and only when truly
  stuck, instead of constraining rotation permanently.

5) DIAGNOSING PHYSICS BUGS WITHOUT BEING ABLE TO DRIVE THE CAR
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Constraint: fixes couldn't be verified by directly playing the game — the workflow
  had to run through the user reproducing an issue and pasting console output back.
- Solution: kept PhysicsDebug.tsx as a live, purpose-built telemetry tool instead of
  guessing blindly — logs per-wheel slip, friction impulse, brake/drive torque,
  ground contact, plus body speed/yaw-rate/pitch/roll.
- Iterated the trigger condition as the bugs changed: first gated to "while braking
  only," later widened to also catch "accelerating hard from near a standstill"
  once a second class of launch bug appeared.
- Every fix in this project followed the same loop: reproduce -> read the actual
  wheel-level numbers -> form a specific hypothesis -> make one targeted change ->
  re-verify against new logs.
- This turned "the car flew away, no idea why" reports into concrete, traceable
  causes (e.g. "one wheel's lng impulse spiked to 3.31 while its neighbors sat
  under 0.3") instead of trial-and-error tuning.

6) FORWARD + HANDBRAKE HELD TOGETHER LAUNCHING THE CAR
------------------------------------------------------------
- Symptom: pressing forward while also holding the handbrake (notably right after
  exiting the project sign zoom-in) sent the car flying forward instead of just
  driving off.
- Root cause: ecctrl's drive controller targets a velocity and keeps commanding
  more torque the longer it's resisted — holding throttle against the (now
  softened) brake let that torque command "wind up," then dump all at once the
  instant it overpowered the brake.
- Fix: in VehicleInputBridge.tsx, brake now always overrides forward/backward
  input — a handbrake and a throttle are never allowed to fight each other.
- Verified fixed by the user directly; no more launch on that specific combo.

7) STANDING-START LAUNCH (WHEELSPIN, THEN LURCH)
------------------------------------------------------
- Symptom: even with no brake involved, flooring the throttle from a dead stop
  produced a violent forward lurch; repeated taps made it worse.
- First hypothesis: TIRE_GRIP was one shared constant across all 4 wheels, but had
  only ever needed lowering on the rear wheels (to soften handbrake locks) — the
  front driven wheels inherited that same reduced grip and couldn't put power down
  cleanly from a stop.
- Split the constant into FRONT_TIRE_GRIP (restored higher) and REAR_TIRE_GRIP
  (kept low) — this helped, but didn't fully resolve the lurch.
- Went back to carConfig and found the actual root cause: engineHorsepower was set
  to 150, while ecctrl's own default is 6 — a 25x mismatch against a physics model
  (gear ratios, torque curves, grip) that's calibrated around that default.
- That single oversized-engine mismatch is very likely the true root cause behind
  most of this session's "launched into the air" bugs, not just this one — every
  wheel-level tuning before it was treating a symptom.
- Dropped engineHorsepower to 14 (still peppier than default, nowhere near 150).
- Widened PhysicsDebug.tsx to also log a [LAUNCH] event whenever accelerating hard
  under ~6 m/s, not just while braking, as a safety net in case it wasn't the
  whole story.
- User confirmed the fix worked well after this change.

8) BILLBOARDS LOOKED MESSY + CAR SLID AWAY WHILE PARKED AT A SIGN
------------------------------------------------------------------------
- User feedback (with screenshot): the project billboard looked like a hazy,
  misaligned blob, and the car kept creeping/sliding while a sign was up.
- Billboard root cause: the picture, title, and the card/glow-rim behind them were
  each positioned independently with mismatched offsets, so the "frame" never
  actually wrapped the content.
- Rewrote ProjectBillboard.tsx with computed layout constants (image and title
  positions derived from actual content size, not eyeballed).
- Made the card and accent rim fully opaque instead of semi-transparent glow — a
  crisp edge instead of a smear.
- Dropped the redundant tagline text from the 3D sign since the 2D overlay panel
  already shows it.
- Retuned FollowCamera's sign-mode look target to aim lower (near the pole base),
  which pushes the (now shorter) sign into the upper part of the frame, clear of
  the panel fixed to the bottom of the screen.
- Stop-sliding root cause: wheel-friction braking alone (already softened for
  handbrake turns) wasn't strong enough to actually hold the car still.
- Fix: while a project sign is active, VehicleInputBridge.tsx now directly zeroes
  the car's horizontal velocity every frame (leaving vertical velocity alone so
  gravity/suspension still settle it naturally) — a hard, immediate stop.
